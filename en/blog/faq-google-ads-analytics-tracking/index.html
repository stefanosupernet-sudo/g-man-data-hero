Google Ads strategy

1. What is Google Ads?

Google Ads is Google's advertising platform that lets businesses show ads to people searching for products, services or information. Its value is not simply generating clicks, but reaching users whose intent matches the company's commercial goals.

2. Does Google Ads really work for businesses?

Yes, but results depend on strategy, market, budget, website, tracking and conversion quality. Google Ads can be highly effective when managed as a customer-acquisition tool—not just a system for buying traffic.

3. How much should you invest in Google Ads?

There is no universal budget. Budget should reflect average click costs, competition, the market, customer value and business goals. A budget Google considers enough to join auctions may still be too low to generate meaningful sales volume.

4. Are €300 or €500 per month enough for Google Ads?

In some markets they can be enough to start, but not necessarily to drive meaningful commercial results. In competitive e-commerce especially, a budget that is too low can limit auctions, data and conversions needed to build an effective strategy.

5. Is Google's recommended budget always right?

No. Google's suggestion must be interpreted in the real market context. The platform can estimate what is needed to participate in auctions, but that does not mean the budget is enough to generate adequate sales.

6. How do you set the right Google Ads budget?

Start from commercial goals, average sale or lead value, sustainable acquisition cost, market demand and competition. Budget should follow strategy—not be the starting point.

7. Is it better to increase budget or optimise campaigns?

It depends on the problem. If a campaign is inefficient, raising budget may simply amplify waste. If campaigns are efficient and the market still has demand, gradually increasing investment can help you scale.

8. Why can a Google Ads campaign get many clicks but few sales?

Causes can include poorly qualified traffic, a non-competitive offer, a weak site, checkout issues, price, low trust or incorrect tracking. Click volume alone does not prove campaign profitability.

9. How do you measure Google Ads campaign success?

It depends on the business model. For e-commerce, sales, conversion value, ROAS, CPA and margin matter. For B2B, qualified leads, cost per lead, meetings and acquired customers are often more important.

10. Is it better to optimise Google Ads for clicks or conversions?

When enough data is available, optimising toward conversions that match commercial goals is usually more useful. The system needs signals that represent the value the business actually wants.

11. What does a Google Ads audit involve?

A Google Ads audit reviews account structure, campaigns, keywords, targeting, ads, conversions, budget, bidding strategies and traffic quality. The goal is to see what works, what wastes budget and which opportunities can be developed.

12. Why analyse a Google Ads account before changing it?

Because changes not based on data can harm an account that already works. Before intervening, you need to understand which campaigns, segments, keywords and conversions actually create value.

13. Should Google Ads be managed the same way for every business?

No. Every company has different margins, customers, competition, sales cycles and goals. An effective strategy must be built on the business model—not applied as a one-size-fits-all template.

14. Which Google Ads campaign types can you use?

Depending on goals, you can use Search, Shopping, Performance Max, Display, Demand Gen and other types available in the platform. Choice should follow user behaviour and the purchase journey.

15. When should you use Google Search campaigns?

Search is especially useful when there is conscious demand and users are actively looking for a product or service. It is often a core component for lead generation and high-intent customer acquisition.

16. What role does Performance Max play?

Performance Max uses Google's automation to distribute ads across multiple Google channels. To work well it needs data, conversion signals, quality assets and a clear commercial strategy.

17. Does Performance Max work without proper conversion setup?

It can run technically, but result quality may suffer. If Google receives the wrong signals, automation can optimise toward goals that do not match the business's real interest.

18. Can Google Ads AI replace a professional?

No. Automation can handle a growing share of operational tasks, but strategy, goal selection, data quality and result interpretation remain essential. An algorithm optimises what it is asked to optimise—if the goal is wrong, it optimises in the wrong direction.

19. Why can Google Ads automation increase errors?

Because automation can quickly amplify the data, signals and settings already in the account. If tracking, conversions, feeds or strategy are wrong, the algorithm may use those inputs to make inefficient decisions.

20. What is the relationship between Google Ads and artificial intelligence?

Google Ads increasingly uses machine learning and automation for bidding, targeting and ad delivery. That is why the quality of data fed into the Google ecosystem has become even more important.

Lead generation and customer acquisition

21. What is lead generation?

Lead generation is the process of acquiring contacts who may be interested in your products or services. A lead can be a form submission, phone call, registration, WhatsApp message or another commercial action.

22. Is Google Ads effective for lead generation?

Yes, especially when you can reach users already looking for a solution. Results depend on keyword quality, commercial offer, the website and correct lead measurement.

23. What is the difference between a lead and a customer?

A lead has shown interest; a customer has completed a commercial transaction. In B2B the path from lead to customer can take weeks or months, so measuring only the final sale is often not enough.

24. Why should B2B track registrations as well?

Because the B2B sales process can take weeks or months. A qualified registration can be an important intent signal and give Google Ads useful data to find similar users.

25. Do all leads have the same value?

No. A generic lead can be worth far less than a genuinely interested, in-target contact. That is why quantity and quality of leads must be distinguished.

26. How do you improve Google Ads lead quality?

Work on keywords, targeting, ad messaging, landing pages, contact qualification and tracking. Connecting ad data to the CRM also helps show which leads become real customers.

27. Can Google Ads be connected to a CRM?

Yes. Integrating Google Ads with a CRM links campaigns not only to generated leads, but also to their quality and, where possible, to commercial value produced.

28. Why is CRM important for B2B Google Ads?

Because it goes beyond the simple 'form filled' metric. A CRM can show which campaigns generate opportunities, meetings, customers and revenue.

29. What is the main mistake in lead generation?

Treating every contact as equal. Generating many leads does not necessarily mean generating many customers.

30. How long does it take to see lead generation results?

It depends on industry, demand, budget, data quality and starting point. With a sound strategy and proper tracking, early improvements can appear in the first months, but stabilisation usually requires an ongoing process.

31. Can a lead generation campaign be scaled?

Yes, when there is a sustainable relationship between investment, cost per lead, contact quality and customer value. Scaling should follow results—not be a simple budget increase.

32. When should you increase a lead generation campaign budget?

When the campaign produces enough qualified leads at a sustainable cost and the business can handle the new contacts. Raising budget without sales capacity can create more contacts without more revenue.

33. Why should calls be counted as conversions?

For many local businesses and professional services, a phone call is one of the most important commercial actions. If it is not tracked, a large share of campaign results stays invisible.

34. Can calls from Google Ads be tracked?

Yes. Calls can be set up as conversions and analysed together with other campaign actions.

35. Is it useful to track WhatsApp as a conversion?

Yes, when WhatsApp is a real customer-acquisition channel. A click on the WhatsApp button can be used as a signal, but ideally you should distinguish a simple chat start from a truly qualified contact.

E-commerce, ROAS and CPA

36. What is ROAS?

ROAS, Return on Ad Spend, measures conversion value generated relative to ad cost. It is very useful for e-commerce, but not always enough to show how much a business actually earns.

37. Does a high ROAS automatically mean a campaign is profitable?

No. A high ROAS can coexist with very thin margins. Profitability also depends on product costs, logistics, returns, fixed costs and other business costs.

38. What is CPA?

CPA, Cost per Acquisition, is how much it costs to get a specific acquisition or conversion. It can be more meaningful than ROAS when the goal is to control the cost of acquiring a customer.

39. When is it better to optimise CPA instead of ROAS?

When business margin depends heavily on acquisition cost. In some e-commerce models—especially with significant fixed costs—reducing CPA can increase profit more than simply raising ROAS.

40. Why can an e-commerce store increase sales but reduce profit?

Because acquiring more customers can require higher ad spend. If margin per order does not grow enough, higher sales may not translate into higher profit.

41. What should you know before optimising Google Ads for e-commerce?

Margins, average order value, fixed costs, sustainable acquisition costs, most profitable products and user behaviour. Without that information you risk optimising campaigns toward goals that do not help the business.

42. Why does margin matter in e-commerce campaigns?

Because revenue is not the same as profit. A campaign should be judged by what the business actually keeps after costs.

43. Can Google Ads work for a small e-commerce store?

Yes, but budget must fit the market and the cost of demand. A smaller store can compete with lower budgets than large players if it carefully chooses products, segments and strategy.

44. Why can an e-commerce store have many carts but few sales?

The issue may be checkout, shipping costs, payment methods, trust, price or purchase-flow complexity. Before increasing ad budget, understand where the journey breaks.

45. Which checkout converts best?

There is no universal rule for every store, but data analysed on this blog shows that a simple, fast checkout can significantly reduce friction versus paths with unnecessary intermediate steps.

46. Why can a complex checkout reduce sales?

Every extra step is a chance to abandon. If users must register, change pages or enter unnecessary information, the risk of dropping off rises.

47. Should users be forced to register before buying?

Not necessarily. If registration adds friction before payment, abandonment can increase. Offering the simplest possible purchase path is often better.

48. Can Google Ads fix a checkout problem?

No. Google Ads can bring qualified users to the site, but it cannot indefinitely compensate for an inefficient purchase process.

49. How do you tell if the problem is Google Ads or the website?

Compare ad data, on-site behaviour, funnel, carts, checkout and sales. If paid traffic shows good engagement but users do not complete purchase, the problem may be after the click.

50. Why is on-site behaviour analysis important for e-commerce?

Because it shows what users actually do after the click. Metrics such as time on site, engagement and scroll can help separate a traffic problem from a user-experience problem.

Google Merchant Center and Google Shopping

51. What is Google Merchant Center?

Google Merchant Center is the platform through which a business shares product data with Google. It is essential for Google Shopping and many product-based e-commerce strategies.

52. Why is the product feed important?

The feed holds the information Google uses to understand and present products. Titles, descriptions, prices, availability, images and other attributes must be accurate and consistent.

53. Can a poorly configured Merchant Center feed hurt campaigns?

Yes. Incomplete information, errors or poorly structured data can reduce how well Google interprets products and therefore campaign efficiency.

54. Why is Merchant Center important for Performance Max?

In e-commerce campaigns the product feed is a core information source. Feed quality affects how well Google's automated systems understand what to sell and to whom.

55. How do you optimise a Google Shopping feed?

Improve titles, descriptions, categories, attributes, images, availability and data structure. Fix errors and use feed rules when needed.

56. Is Google Merchant Center just a product archive?

No. It is a strategic part of Google's e-commerce ecosystem. Proper Merchant Center management can directly affect Shopping and Performance Max campaign quality.

57. Why are product titles and descriptions important?

They help Google understand the product and its relevance to user searches. More precise information can improve how the system matches the product to the right demand.

58. Can AI automatically optimise the feed?

Automation can help in several areas, but strategic oversight remains important. The feed must correctly represent products, commercial positioning and real store information.

59. Why should a Google Ads agency also handle Merchant Center?

Because campaigns and feeds are tightly linked in e-commerce. Completely separating advertising from product data makes it harder to find the real causes of performance.

60. When should you restructure Merchant Center?

When the feed has errors, incomplete information, uncompetitive products or a structure that prevents campaigns from using available data effectively.

Google Maps and local SEO

61. Why is Google Maps important for a local business?

Because it reaches users searching for businesses, products or services in a specific geographic area. For many local businesses, Google Maps can become a real customer-acquisition channel.

62. What is a Google Business Profile?

It is the profile that represents a business on Google Search and Google Maps. It can include address, hours, phone, website, photos, reviews and other useful information for users.

63. Is Google Maps only for showing an address?

No. An optimised profile can generate calls, direction requests, website visits, messages and appointments.

64. How do you optimise a Google Business Profile?

Keep business data accurate, refine categories and information, update content, manage reviews and analyse user interaction data.

65. Do reviews affect local presence?

Reviews contribute to reputation and influence user trust. Reputation management should therefore be part of local marketing strategy.

66. Can Google Maps generate phone calls?

Yes. The profile can let users call the business directly. Calls should therefore be treated as a possible conversion in analysis.

67. Can Google Maps generate customers without Google Ads?

Yes. Organic local presence can bring users searching directly for a business or service. Google Ads can still complement and amplify presence in strategic areas.

68. Why do brand-name searches matter?

When users start searching for the business name directly, it can signal growing awareness and trust in the brand.

69. What is local SEO?

Local SEO covers activities that improve visibility in geographically relevant searches, including those tied to Google Maps and the Google Business Profile.

70. How do you measure the value of Google Maps?

Do not look at views alone. It is more useful to analyse concrete actions such as calls, website visits, direction requests, messages and other contacts.

GA4, tracking and conversions

71. Why is conversion tracking so important?

Because it shows which marketing activities produce results. Without reliable tracking, it is hard to separate traffic that creates value from traffic that only produces visits.

72. What is Google Analytics 4?

Google Analytics 4 is the analytics platform for collecting and interpreting data on user behaviour on websites and apps.

73. What does correctly configuring GA4 mean?

It means defining which events and actions should be measured, how they should be classified and which ones truly represent business goals.

74. What is an event in GA4?

An event is an action a user takes—such as a page view, click, form submit, purchase or another interaction configured in the system.

75. What is the difference between an event and a conversion?

An event records an interaction. A conversion is an event considered especially important for the business goal.

76. Why should you not mark every event as a conversion?

Because Google Ads needs meaningful signals. If low-value actions are used as conversions, the algorithm may optimise toward users who take easy actions that are not commercially useful.

77. Which conversions should a business track?

It depends on the business. Examples include purchases, forms, calls, registrations, appointments, quote requests, WhatsApp messages or other actions truly tied to customer acquisition.

78. Can you have primary and secondary conversions?

Yes. It is useful to separate conversions that should drive automated optimisation from those used mainly for analysis and deeper insight.

79. Why can a tracking error damage Google Ads?

Because automated strategies use conversion data to make decisions. If data is incomplete or wrong, optimisations can become less effective.

80. Should Google Ads and GA4 be linked?

Linking them is often very useful because it combines advertising data with behavioural data, making it easier to analyse the user journey and conversions.

Consent Mode, privacy and GDPR

81. What is Google Consent Mode?

Google Consent Mode lets Google tags adjust their behaviour based on the consent preferences expressed by the user.

82. What is Consent Mode V2?

Consent Mode V2 is the evolution of Google's consent framework, with additional signals related to use of data for advertising and personalisation.

83. What are the main Consent Mode V2 signals?

Key signals include ad_user_data and ad_personalization, which relate to using personal data for advertising purposes and to ad personalisation.

84. Does Consent Mode affect Google Ads?

Yes. Consent can influence advertising features such as remarketing, measurement and use of data to optimise campaigns.

85. Does Consent Mode also affect GA4?

Yes. The system is also relevant to how data is collected and used in Google Analytics 4.

86. Is a cookie banner with only an Accept button enough?

A modern consent system should let users make genuinely informed choices, including the ability to reject or customise certain cookie and tracking categories.

87. Why should the cookie banner be connected to the data layer?

Because user choices must be communicated correctly to tracking systems. The data layer can connect consent, tags and analytics tools.

88. What happens if a user rejects marketing cookies?

Tracking must respect that choice and not use data for purposes that require that consent. Technical setup must reflect the preferences expressed.

89. Is Consent Mode only for GDPR compliance?

No. Beyond the legal aspect, correct implementation can help preserve some measurement and optimisation capability in the Google ecosystem when users do not grant certain consents.

90. Who should configure Consent Mode?

It requires privacy, tracking and technical implementation skills. Many projects therefore coordinate legal/privacy professionals with analytics and tag-management specialists.

Attribution, AI and agency collaboration

91. What is attribution in digital marketing?

Attribution is the process of analysing how different channels and touchpoints contribute to the user journey before conversion.

92. Why should you not attribute a sale only to the last click?

Because the user may have interacted with multiple channels before converting. Google Ads, Meta, organic search, referral, email and direct traffic can play different roles in the same journey.

93. What are touchpoints?

They are the different contact points between user and brand during the customer journey—ads, organic results, social, email, referral, direct visits and other channels.

94. Can Google Ads and Meta work together?

Yes. Google Ads and Meta can play different roles in acquisition. Evaluating the two channels separately, without the full customer journey, can lead to wrong conclusions.

95. Why should a business analyse the customer journey?

To understand how users reach conversion and which channels contribute to the decision. That helps allocate budget and marketing activity more effectively.

96. Can artificial intelligence replace marketing analysis?

No. Tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini and other AI systems can help process information, compare scenarios and speed up some tasks, but analysis quality depends on data quality and the ability to interpret it.

97. How can a digital marketing agency work with a Google specialist?

The agency can keep the client relationship and broader marketing activities, while the specialist focuses on Google Ads, tracking, Merchant Center, analytics or specific technical issues.

98. Why should an agency rely on an external Google specialist?

Not every agency has deep in-house expertise on Google Ads, tracking, GA4 and Merchant Center. A specialist can complement the agency without necessarily replacing it.

99. What should a business look for in a Google Ads professional?

Experience, analytical skill, business understanding, tracking quality, reporting transparency and the ability to explain decisions. The professional should not only 'manage campaigns' but help the business understand what is actually producing results.

100. Why choose a specialised Google professional instead of standardised management?

Because Google Ads, Analytics, Merchant Center and tracking must be adapted to the business model. A specialist approach starts from data, goals and market, building a strategy that aims to generate commercial value—not just traffic.
